Where Stash sits in the AI-memory market — and where I fit

Farzan Aman Khan  ·  github.com/cheese-cakee  ·  July 2026

Supermemory and Mem0 sell memory technology to developers — invisible plumbing inside someone else's app. Stash gives whole teams a shared brain: one knowledge base that people and AI agents read and write together. That is a different market, and the more valuable one.

The market at a glance

Stash has the top-right quadrant to itself: team-wide knowledge, in a form humans can actually read.

Supermemory and Mem0 compete with each other — and with every AI lab that adds built-in memory to its models. Stash doesn't sit in that fight. It owns something none of them capture: the working history of a team and its agents — what was tried, what was decided, and why.

Three reasons the Stash approach wins

1

Agents already speak its language

Stash presents itself as a filesystem — the thing coding agents use all day, every day. No new interface to learn, no integration project. Adoption friction is close to zero.

2

Memory you can open and fix

When the other tools remember something wrong, nobody can see it — the error surfaces later, silently, in the AI's answers. In Stash a wrong memory is just a file: open it, correct it, done. For companies, that transparency is what makes AI memory trustworthy enough to buy.

3

It captures what nobody else does

Stash records what a team's agents attempted, decided, and abandoned. The most expensive knowledge in any engineering team is why the last attempt failed — and Stash is the only product of the three that keeps it.
