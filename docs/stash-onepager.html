Where Stash sits in the AI memory market, and where I fit

Farzan Aman Khan  ·  github.com/cheese-cakee  ·  July 2026

Supermemory and Mem0 sell memory infrastructure to developers building AI apps. It lives inside someone else's product, and the end user never sees it. Stash sells something else: one knowledge base that a whole team and its AI agents share. Different buyer, different product, and in my view the stronger position.

The market at a glance

Stash has the top-right quadrant to itself: knowledge the whole team shares, in a form people can actually read.

Supermemory and Mem0 compete with each other, and with every AI lab that keeps adding built-in memory to its models. Stash is not in that fight. It keeps something none of them do: the record of what a team's agents tried, what got decided, and why.

Three reasons the Stash approach wins

1

Agents already speak its language

Coding agents spend their whole day running ls, find and grep. Stash looks like a filesystem, so they can use it from the first minute. There is no new interface to teach and no integration project to sell.

2

Memory you can open and fix

When Supermemory or Mem0 store a wrong fact, it sits in a database until the AI gives a bad answer. In Stash a wrong memory is a file. Someone opens it, fixes it, and moves on. Companies need that kind of visibility before they will trust a memory product with real work.

3

It keeps what nobody else does

Stash records what a team's agents attempted, what worked, and what got abandoned. Ask any engineer what they most wish they knew: it is usually why the last attempt failed. The other two keep distilled facts; Stash keeps the full record.
